What’s new in 3.0

Public Beta v3

Apple Music's new Android design

A floating glass tab bar and mini player, titles with a round ⋯ on one line, rebuilt artist and album pages, and Settings grouped into cards, all matched to Apple Music for Android 7.0.

Themes that keep their colour

Type a colour code key by key, pick from a real colour picker, see light and dark side by side, and your accent shows everywhere, exactly the colour you chose.

Deeper, more honest Sing

More vocal removal at the top of the slider, a fuller backing between lines, a clear message on mono recordings, and no more blips when you turn it on or off. Still entirely on your phone.

Instant tabs, lighter on older phones

Going back to a tab is instant. Older and low-memory phones skip the heavy effects, play fewer videos at once and give memory back when Octave is in the background.

The new look
Apple Music for Android 7.0 redesigned the app, and Octave now follows it. The tab bar and the mini player float above the page as glass pills, sized and placed like Apple's, with real blur on Android 12 and newer. The selected tab is shown by its colour alone. On album, playlist and artist pages both pills take on the artwork's colour. Page titles sit on one line with a round ⋯ button; scroll down and only the ⋯ stays. The search field is squarer and stays pinned at the top while you scroll. Song rows use Apple's vertical ⋮.
Artist and album pages
Artists open on a square photo that melts into the artist's colour, with the name and three buttons, info, play and favourite, right under it. The photo drifts, fades and blurs as you scroll, and stretches when you pull down. Square album covers now sit inset with a soft shadow on a flat page, while albums with a tall animated cover still fill the screen. Playlists use the same design. Back, Share and ⋯ are separate round buttons. Song Details shows the cover as a centred card with rounded corners instead of running edge to edge, and Credits is a full page with View Lyrics.
Library, Search and Settings
Library puts New Playlist and ⋯ on the title line, and Edit Pins and Edit Categories live in the ⋯ menu. Settings is grouped into cards like Apple Music's, with a centred title. Theme now has a System option that follows your phone; Dark stays the default.
Motion and controls
Tabs cross-fade in 150 ms, and Back from the top of a tab returns to the tab you came from. Going back to a tab you already visited is instant: the page is kept instead of being rebuilt. Menus open as solid sheets, dialogs use text buttons, switches match Apple's, and dragging songs to reorder ticks under your finger. The app opens on its icon.
Sing
Sing goes deeper at the top of the slider and keeps the backing music fuller between sung lines. Rap vocals are handled better. On mono and near-mono recordings it now says "This mix can't be separated" instead of hollowing out the song. Turning Sing on or off no longer repeats or skips a sliver of audio, song endings are no longer clipped, and lyrics stay in time while it is on. When the phone runs hot or Battery Saver is on, Sing eases off and tells you. Everything still runs on your phone.
Older and low-memory phones
On Android 11 and older the glass skips the live blur, as Apple Music does. On low-end phones, Home's animated covers become still images, fewer videos play at once, caches are sized to the phone's memory and released when Octave goes to the background. If the system closes Octave while it is playing, Share Logs now records it, so these reports can be traced.
Smaller fixes
Top Songs opened from a link no longer shows the wrong artist's name. Search's typing view, the selection bar and the Jump to current song button no longer sit under the mini player. Radio keeps its scroll position when Android restores the app. Opening Now Playing from a coloured mini player no longer flashes grey. Toasts and album rows match Apple's.
Known limits in this build
Live blur needs Android 12 or newer, and low-end phones skip it. Dolby Atmos stays off Bluetooth headphones on Automatic; choose Always On if yours handle it. Some phones make faint clicks over Bluetooth with certain headphones, and other apps on the same phone do it too; turning on "Disable Bluetooth A2DP hardware offload" in Developer options can help. This is a beta: if something breaks, tell us in the Discord.

A file from TuneMyMusic or Exportify used to come back with a hundred or more songs "not in the catalog", and most of them were there — the matcher was being rate-limited and counted every refusal as a miss. Skip works on stations again, "& Similar Artists" no longer starts on the same song every time, and the iPhone app stays in portrait.

  4. 7.2

    September 19, 2026

    AutoMix no longer rewrites the song that is playing: every upcoming song is now created with its own transition already inside it, so when the seam comes the mixer only sets two timestamps. That was the short dropout a few seconds into a song on iPhone. A song that lost its stream and whose replacement never loaded used to spin forever; play rebuilds it from a fresh source now.

  5. 7.1

    September 19, 2026

    Songs stuttered in their first minute for two reasons that are now fixed: with Repeat One on, the song about to loop was downloaded again on every lap while it played, and editing the queue during AutoMix rewrote the live song's audio mix three times in a row. The whole app also ran at 60 Hz on ProMotion iPhones — it now runs at the display's full rate.

AutoMix now blends on a phone instead of cutting — the filter that carries the fade was never being scheduled, so every beat-matched transition was a hard cut. Apple's redesigned artist page is here, Siri can open your library by name, and Podcasts has been rebuilt.

  27. 3.8

    August 8, 2026

    This is the loudness release. Octave has been misreading the loudness figure Deezer publishes for every track — it says how loud a master already is, and Octave was applying it as if it were a correction, which pushed every song twice as far from the target as it should have gone. Modern pop masters sit close enough to that target to have come out roughly right, which is how this went unnoticed for so long; older and quieter records did not, and some were playing nearly 19 dB below where another service plays the same song. Also here: your public profile is finally reachable from the account menu instead of only from deep inside Settings.

  28. 3.7

    August 6, 2026

    A small diagnostic release. Widgets that have nothing to show now print the build that drew them, so a screenshot of a misbehaving widget says which version is actually running. There is no way to tell that otherwise, and on a sideloaded install it is genuinely often the answer — the update didn't apply, or a widget placed before a re-sign is still being served by the old extension.

  29. 3.6

    August 6, 2026

    Three fixes, all reported from real installs. The Lock Screen widget was rendering a grey "Please adopt containerBackground API" card instead of your music, because iOS will not draw a widget that doesn't declare its background and the Lock Screen sizes never declared one. Opening the app from a web link could throw an "Import Failed" alert about a theme you never tried to import. And links copied out of the theme gallery quietly did nothing when opened.

  30. 3.5

    August 6, 2026

    Every Home Screen widget has been rebuilt on the system's own background. The old ones painted their own dark surface and washed it with the album's colour, which turned all six the same muddy maroon — and because the system's secondary text colours are calculated against the system's background rather than one an app invents, artist names and section headers ended up sitting at almost exactly the contrast of the surface they were printed on. Handing the background back to iOS fixes the readability, brings light mode with it, and lets iOS 26 render the widgets as Liquid Glass.

  31. 3.4

    August 6, 2026

    Downloading a list could get stuck circling one song — 100%, back to 90%, up to 100% again, then a failure — while the rest of the queue waited. The song was rarely the problem. Nothing in the server's reply said how large a download was meant to be, so Octave guessed the size from the bitrate, could not tell a file that finished from one that stopped early, and started the whole thing over from byte one after every interruption. Downloads now carry their real size, are checked on arrival, and can be picked up from wherever they stopped.

  32. 3.3

    August 6, 2026

    Tapping Download on a long list could end with one song being attempted forever while everything behind it waited. A track the servers genuinely can't deliver now gets a limited number of tries, then steps aside for a while instead of being handed a clean slate every few minutes — and Octave tells you it happened, rather than leaving you to notice the gap days later.

  33. 3.2

    August 5, 2026

    A follow-up to 3.1's widget fix that takes the guesswork out of it. 3.1 searched for the App Group identifier it had been issued by trying the names a signing tool plausibly uses; 3.2 reads the actual name out of the provisioning profile the signer embedded in the app, so it works whatever the tool decided to call it.

  34. 3.1

    August 5, 2026

    Home-Screen widgets work on a normal install. They read the app's data through an App Group, and an App Group identifier belongs permanently to whoever registered it — so a copy signed with your own Apple ID was handed a different one, or none at all, and every widget came up empty for everyone who wasn't building the app themselves. Octave now finds whichever group it was actually given. Where there is none, the three widgets that don't need your library load from our servers instead of sitting blank.

  35. 3.0

    August 5, 2026

    An artwork release. The player was drawing a blurred copy of the album cover behind the real one and dissolving the real one halfway down, which is why the whole screen read as out of focus — both layers are gone. Covers also stopped being the wrong album, and colour now comes from the picture actually on screen.

  36. 2.10
